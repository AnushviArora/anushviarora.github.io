Workflow redesign

Every show was reserving equipment it would never use. I gave the system a choice.

Resource management booking path With one teammate · about 6 weeks

FOX's booking system treated every broadcast the same way. Each event automatically reserved encoders, decoders and a resource management license, even daily shows that already had their own dedicated gear. That meant duplicate equipment sitting locked away, and licenses spent on events that never needed them.

What I did
  • Reframed it as a routing problem. My teammate and I designed a second path, so streams that don't need resource management skip it entirely, and everything else runs exactly as before.
  • Put the decision where it belongs. I rebuilt 4 automation scripts so operators make the call in one click, right when they book.
  • Changed the system without changing the habits. New bookings move through the same lifecycle operators already knew. Getting there meant cracking the hardest bug of the project, status buttons wired deep into my scripts.
  • Tried to break it 75+ times before anyone else could. Bookings with and without resource management, events shut down mid-stream, every status in between, then code review with our manager.

Product design and build

Operators were fighting their captioning tool. I rebuilt it around how they actually work.

LEXI captioning control app Solo · about a month, alongside the booking project

Operators control AI captioning across 100+ live streams through AI-Media's LEXI, working from touch panels. Their tool was a low-code app where past, live and upcoming bookings were jumbled together, basic options were missing, and nothing was designed for touch.

What I did
  • Designed for the hands actually using it. A swipeable stream bar for touch panels, with dropdown and search for when speed matters.
  • Made it impossible to take a show off air by accident. Switching to the backup feed turns off an idle main automatically, and a feed carrying a live broadcast can't be switched at all.
  • Turned wasted spend into a feature. FOX pays for every minute LEXI captions, so any stream left on with no input raises an alarm, counts down from 5 minutes and shuts itself off.
  • Made the whole week visible at once. Calendar and list views, with bookings that set language, caption rows and time zone in one place.
  • Shipped it like a real product. Sign-in with existing accounts, staging and production environments, and FOX's brand throughout.

AI product · In progress

Auto-captions get the most important words wrong. Cuewise checks them against the professor's slides.

Cuewise Solo builder and product owner

Lecture auto-captions stumble on exactly the vocabulary students need to learn. For students who are deaf or hard of hearing, studying in a second language, or just reviewing for an exam, the most important words in a lecture are the least reliable ones.

What I'm building
  • Captions grounded in the source. Every caption is checked against the professor's own slides, so technical terms come out right.
  • No silent fixes. Every correction is shown for review. In accessibility, trust matters more than polish.
  • Built for real lectures, not demos. It handles full 40-minute recordings with chunking, retries and caching.
  • Next up. Class links for students, "Ask this lecture" for studying, and a confusion map that shows professors exactly where students got lost.

Voice AI · Side project

Talking to my Mac should feel like talking to a person. So I built the assistant I wanted.

Oreo Solo builder

Oreo lives on your desktop as a small floating bubble. Say its name and ask for anything. "Play Blank Space on YouTube." "Draft an email in Gmail." "Turn the volume down to 30%." It talks back, keeps a conversation going and remembers what you tell it.

Product decisions
  • Speed is the product. Replies play while they're still being generated, Oreo knows you're done after 0.75 seconds of silence, and everyday chat uses minimal reasoning so it never feels sluggish.
  • Private by default. The wake word is detected on your Mac, memory stays on the device, and the API key lives in the Keychain.
  • Actions, not just answers. It drives Safari, YouTube, Gmail, Spotify and system settings directly.

AI shopping · Sponsored capstone · In progress

You know what you want the moment you see it. Shopful is built so a picture can be the search.

Shopful Project lead and UI/UX owner · 5-person team Try the interactive prototype ↗

You see an outfit you love but can't describe it. You search five websites and still don't know if anything similar is sold nearby. Shopful brings inspiration, products and local stores into one experience, and helps local stores get found by the people already looking for what they sell.

What I own
  • The bridge between vision and build. I run the relationship with our sponsor and turn her feedback into clear priorities a five-person team across design, frontend, backend, AI and research can actually execute.
  • A calmer way to shop. Early concepts felt loud and crowded. I pushed the product toward softer color, subtle depth, generous spacing and readable type, because browsing should feel easy, not overwhelming.
  • Deciding by using, not debating. I built two fully interactive concepts, a pastel "cloud" direction and a darker "flower" direction, so the team chose by clicking through real experiences. Cloud won.
  • A handoff built to survive implementation. Prototype, source files, visual specs, interaction notes and a UI testing checklist, so the design that ships is the design we chose.
  • Never letting a blocker stall the product. When account access held up integration, I built a sample-data prototype so design decisions kept moving while the backend caught up.
